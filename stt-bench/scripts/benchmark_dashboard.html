How to read this benchmark

One combined accuracy score. Add substitutions, insertions, and deletions across Pipecat, FLEURS, and private recordings, then divide by their total reference words. Each word has equal weight; datasets with more words contribute more. We do not average dataset percentages. Successful recoveries can improve final accuracy.

Coverage is part of the result. The English suite contains 1,000 Pipecat clips, 180 FLEURS clips, and 8 private recordings. Nova-3 has the full FLEURS sample; Gradium and Reson8 have 15 each. Other models have no saved FLEURS measurement. Missing clips add neither errors nor words. A low score on less data is not proof of a better model.

What receives a rank. Completed runs with all Pipecat and private items attempted and usable results in both datasets receive a rank. Failed or excluded items stay visible in coverage. Chirp 3 has private results only; Sarvam and Soniox have small trials only. These models remain unranked. AssemblyAI receives a rank when its full run and evidence checks are complete. Trial Pipecat duplicates and private excerpts are not added on top of full runs.

Model selection. Flux uses English. Nova-2, Flux Multilingual, Whisper, GPT-4o Mini, and Chirp 2 are omitted. GPT-4o Transcribe is the single OpenAI entry. Nova-3 and Flux remain distinct model families; the two Speechmatics operating modes remain visible.

p50, p90, p95, p99. Respectively, 50%, 90%, 95%, and 99% of observations fall at or below these values, using linear interpolation. Percentiles are calculated from individual observations, never averages of dataset or worker percentiles. Sparse samples make tail percentiles less reliable.

Interim timing. The saved metric is the first nonempty partial update after speech ends. It is not time to the first transcript from speech start. “Unavailable” means no eligible observation; it does not mean the model cannot stream interim text. Public timing pools Pipecat and FLEURS first attempts that passed validity checks.

Final and word timing. Public final-transcript delay ends when the last final-text event arrives, relative to speech end; it can be negative if final text arrived earlier. Private finalized-word delay measures receipt of correctly aligned final words relative to delivery of their word-end audio. These are different measurements and are never pooled together. Stream completion additionally waits for the provider's completion signal.

Limits and evidence. Network time is included. Runs used different dates and concurrency settings. Gradium, Gemini, and Chirp use session handoffs for long recordings; AssemblyAI uses 60 ms packets. References and word boundaries were not independently checked by listening. This page uses saved scores, not live progress. No confidence intervals or overall accuracy-plus-speed score are invented.
